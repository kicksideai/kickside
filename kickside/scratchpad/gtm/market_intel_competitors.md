# Market Intelligence competitors (2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-07-10. Input: `impl/market_intel_addendum.md`.

## Executive read

The category has split into four useful product patterns:

1. **TAM and market mapping:** Ocean.io, Keyplay, CB Insights, and 6sense start with a market/ICP model, lookalikes, categories, or a company graph.
2. **Continuous signal delivery:** Bombora, Apollo, Common Room, Koala, and ZoomInfo turn web, intent, jobs, news, funding, technology, and engagement activity into account-level alerts or scores.
3. **Programmable enrichment and research:** Clay and Pocus combine provider waterfalls with AI/web research, then let an operator turn results into lists, scores, or plays.
4. **Activation systems:** nearly all of them sync to a CRM, sequence tool, Slack, ads, or an API. Most optimize for “make the next action happen,” not for a durable, inspectable research history.

The design implication is not to copy one vendor. Copy the good primitives, but keep the component’s stronger boundary: digestion, scanning, deep scanning, and sourcing are background work over one evolving market view; factual state is thread events; prose and synthesis live in one market KB; queryable funnel/seen state is a projection; and only candidate rows enter the existing import → review conveyor.

### What the public product material does—and does not—show

The products below demonstrate strong identity resolution, recurring refresh, multi-signal synthesis, and CRM activation. Public documentation rarely describes all of these together:

- a source-independent company seen-registry shared by every discovery path;
- an explicit `sourced / parked / rejected` state machine that suppresses repeat proposals;
- rejection rationale retained as searchable memory and used by the next scan;
- a first-class, append-only provenance thread spanning scans, deep scans, sourcing, and human decisions.

That absence is the opportunity. It is also a warning: a persistent table, CRM sync, or alert history is not automatically semantic memory or incremental discovery.

## 2. Capability findings that matter to our design

### Market mapping: “whole picture” is a real product surface

Ocean’s segments show cohort performance and untouched-in-CRM whitespace; Keyplay combines categories, lookalikes, and signal models; 6sense has a Company Graph and buying groups; CB Insights provides market maps, strategy maps, relationships, and market-level monitoring. These are the strongest precedents for making the market component more than a lead list.

**Adopt:** the detail view should show the market digest, segment/cluster map, coverage gaps, and a funnel—not just the latest scan results. Preserve a time series so John can see whether the market is expanding, contracting, or simply being reclassified.

### Cadence is usually attached to a monitor, audience, or field

The concrete cadence patterns are useful and varied:

- Clay: look-back period + recurrence frequency per signal; auto-update and conditional runs for enrichments.
- Common Room: daily job/news refresh and automated segment alerts.
- Koala: real-time first-party events and signal-triggered actions.
- Apollo: weekly intent capture, scheduled CRM enrichment, and workflows.
- Keyplay: weekly job-post scraping, quarterly static-page scraping, user-scheduled agents.
- 6sense: daily intent-score update.
- Bombora: relative historical baselines and surge/journey stages.

**Adopt:** separate `digest_cadence`, `scan_cadence`, and `deep_scan_policy`; record the schedule and run watermark in component state. A daily scan should not imply a weekly prose digest or an expensive deep scan of every account.

### Signal taxonomies are more useful when they carry time and evidence

The best taxonomies are not just “intent.” Keyplay’s Profile / Org / Relevance / Stack split is a clean starting shape. Clay and Pocus show the breadth of useful triggers: funding, hiring, job change, tech change, web/product engagement, news, leadership, and custom AI signals. Bombora and Apollo add frequency, trend, recency, surge, topic, and buying-stage semantics. Common Room and Koala show the value of first-party/product/community events alongside third-party signals.

**Adopt:** normalize every finding into a signal event with at least:

`signal_kind`, `subject_key`, `observed_at`, `first_seen_at`, `last_seen_at`, `source`, `source_url`, `raw_evidence_ref`, `strength`, `novelty`, `confidence`, and `expires_at`.

The model should preserve raw evidence and source attribution, then let the digest agent synthesize. A score without the underlying event stack is not an adequate explanation.

### Deep enrichment is moving from lookup to judgment

Claygent and Keyplay agents browse public pages and job posts for last-mile facts. Pocus agents synthesize account priorities and compelling events. Apollo and Clay waterfalls solve the coverage/verification problem by cascading providers and stopping when a usable result is found. Common Room’s Person360/Account Profile and 6sense’s buying-group graph show that enrichment is increasingly relationship-aware.

**Adopt:** make deep scan a bounded, evidence-producing job: resolve identity, enrich the company, find relevant people, inspect current web/news/jobs/technology evidence, produce a cited dossier, and emit only a delta plus a recommendation. Do not let the agent directly create CRM records.

### Dedup is strongest at identity resolution and weakest at decision memory

Good identity precedents:

- Koala: domain-keyed accounts, enrichment-based multi-domain merge, email-keyed visitors.
- Keyplay: unique final domain plus verified domain↔LinkedIn relationship.
- Common Room: Person360 and unified Organizations.
- 6sense: Company Graph and buying-group identity resolution.
- Clay: persistent account/contact profiles in Audiences.

Apollo’s public duplicate docs are the useful counterexample: even with merge tooling, duplicates can still exist. These products mostly prevent or clean up identity duplicates; they do not publicly describe “this company was already proposed by Ocean/Clay/our prospecting request and is now suppressed everywhere.”

**Adopt:** create one component-owned company/market-entity seen-registry keyed domain-first, with aliases and source refs. Make it a projection over thread events, not a mutable fact hidden in an agent table. Every source—including round-1 prospecting requests—must reserve/check the same key before sourcing.

### Incremental discovery is not the same as “latest feed”

The category’s incremental techniques are:

- compare against a historical baseline (Bombora surge, 6sense daily behavior model);
- persist signal columns/records and reuse them across segments (Clay);
- use account profiles and intent history (Common Room, Apollo);
- use rolling scrape schedules and cohort-relative signals (Keyplay);
- trigger only on new first-party events (Koala);
- filter “net new” against a current database/CRM (Apollo, Ocean).

These are valuable, but none of the reviewed public surfaces clearly combines source-independent novelty, operator decisions, rejection rationale, and resurfacing only on new evidence. That is the exact role of `sourced / parked / rejected` plus a `last_evidence_fingerprint` or equivalent registry projection.

### “What do we know about X?” exists, but usually as an account profile

Common Room Account Profiles are the closest direct precedent: CRM, contacts, signal activity, AI research, job listings, news, scoring, and firmographics in one view. Apollo exposes intent history; Koala exposes raw activity and signal highlights; Keyplay generates AI account research; Pocus offers unified account intelligence; 6sense explains “why this account, why now”; CB Insights supplies company/market monitoring and graphs.

**Adopt:** build a market-level and company-level dossier from the market KB, with explicit sections for current understanding, changes since the prior scan, evidence, open questions, and prior human decisions. The KB is prose/semantic memory; the thread is factual event history; the seen-registry/funnel/timeline are projections. Do not turn the KB into the dedup database.

### CRM activation is mature; review-first sourcing is not the default

Common patterns are automatic write-back, list/sequence enrollment, Slack alerts, CRM field mapping, or workflow branching. Apollo is a useful exception because workflow enrollment approval can create manual review tasks before actions begin. Keyplay’s CSV/CRM-ID export is more operator-mediated. Clay’s tables/audiences provide a natural review surface, but actions can still run automatically. Koala’s Actions engine is explicitly built to import/update CRM records as signals fire.

**Adopt:** preserve the import conveyor as the system boundary: scan/deep scan emits candidate rows with provenance; an import session performs alias/company dedup and qualification; John reviews/parks/rejects/merges; only approved rows promote. The market component may auto-record evidence and update projections, but it should not bypass review for new lead sourcing.

## 4. ANTI-PATTERNS

- **The black-box surge score.** A score without topic, source, time window, baseline, evidence URL, and matched events cannot support good review or learning.
- **Re-running a saved search as “continuous intelligence.”** It finds the same companies again and shifts dedup work onto John. Continuous means incremental state plus a novelty decision.
- **One table/list per source.** Ocean, Clay, prospecting requests, and web scans must converge on one company identity and seen-registry, or duplicates are inevitable.
- **Mixing semantic memory with operational state.** A KB is not a uniqueness constraint; a seen-registry is not a place to store the entire research narrative.
- **Permanent rejection with no evidence rule.** “Rejected” should suppress the same evidence, not suppress a company forever. Resurface only on new evidence or an explicit operator request.
- **Automatic CRM creation on first signal.** A signal is not a lead, and an account-level signal often has no person identity. Emit a candidate row and let import/review decide.
- **Deep-scanning every account.** Broad scans need cheap checks; deep research belongs behind fit/novelty/multi-signal gates and should carry cost/latency metadata.
- **Treating all signals as equal.** A page view, a funding announcement, a new VP, and a job post mentioning a target technology have different recency, reliability, and actionability.
- **No account-level identity boundary.** Person alias reservations alone do not dedup companies, subsidiaries, former domains, or brand/legal-name variants.
- **Discarding negative decisions.** Park/reject reasons are valuable market knowledge and should be searchable, cited, and available to the next agent.
- **No source freshness or observation time.** “Company is hiring” is not a fact without when it was observed, how many roles, and whether the job is still open.
- **Letting agents mutate projections directly.** Agents should emit events/findings; reducers/projections own seen state, funnel counts, and timeline consistency.

## 5. Differentiator angle

Competitors are very good at finding, enriching, scoring, and activating accounts. Their default experience is a feed, audience, table, score, alert, or CRM write-back—often with an account profile and some signal history, but rarely with a shared proposal ledger and durable human decision memory. Our differentiator is the combination of a **HITL import → review conveyor** and **thread/event provenance**: every digest, scan finding, deep-scan result, source attempt, park/reject decision, merge, and promotion is an event in the market component’s own thread; the KB answers the semantic “what do we know?” question; projections make dedup, funnel, and timeline queries fast; and the shared seen-registry prevents every future cadence and discovery source from re-proposing the same company. That makes background intelligence cumulative and inspectable rather than an opaque stream of fresh-looking leads.
